These Property Verification and Physical Inspection Terms apply when a Landlord asks RentDirect to verify a Listing through an in-person property inspection and document review instead of uploading property ownership documents. They supplement the Terms of Service, Privacy Policy, Verification and Screening Consent, Marketplace and Rental Disclaimer, Acceptable Use Policy, Landlord Listing and Property Owner Terms, and Subscription and Payment Policy.

## 1. THE TWO VERIFICATION OPTIONS

For each Listing, RentDirect may make available one of the following property-verification routes:

* **Document route:** the Landlord uploads the requested property documents through the Platform for review; or
* **Physical inspection route:** the Landlord opts in to an authorised RentDirect agent or qualified lawyer visiting the property, inspecting the visible property, and reviewing the relevant documents made available during or in connection with the visit.

The Landlord must select the route honestly and must not represent that a property has passed verification while the relevant status is pending or unverified.

## 2. REQUEST AND CONSENT

By selecting physical or in-person verification, submitting a Listing, confirming an inspection appointment, or allowing an Inspector to enter the property, the Landlord:

* requests the physical inspection and document-review service for the identified Listing;
* authorises RentDirect and its authorised Inspector to collect, review, record, and retain information reasonably required for that service;
* confirms that the Landlord owns the property or has current authority from the owner to request the inspection and arrange the letting;
* confirms that the Landlord may lawfully give the Inspector access to the relevant areas and documents; and
* agrees to cooperate with reasonable identity, authority, scheduling, safety, and clarification requests.

The Landlord must obtain any permission required from an owner, co-owner, corporate principal, property manager, tenant, occupant, estate manager, security provider, or other person whose rights may be affected by the visit.

## 3. APPOINTMENT OF THE INSPECTOR

RentDirect may appoint an employee, contractor, agent, property professional, or qualified lawyer as the Inspector. RentDirect may replace an Inspector, use more than one Inspector, or require a second visit or additional review where reasonable.

The Inspector should identify themselves and, where applicable, show a RentDirect authorisation or appointment reference. The Landlord must not pay cash, share an OTP or password, or provide a personal payment credential to an Inspector unless a payment has been separately and securely confirmed through RentDirect’s approved process.

An Inspector is not authorised to change the Listing price, accept rent, collect a deposit, sign a tenancy agreement for the Landlord, promise approval, or make a legal commitment for RentDirect or the Landlord unless a separate written authority expressly permits it.

## 4. SCHEDULING AND ACCESS

The Landlord must provide accurate contact, address, location, access, and availability information and must make the property reasonably accessible at the agreed time. The Landlord or an authorised representative should be present unless RentDirect expressly agrees otherwise.

The Landlord must:

* disclose access restrictions, occupants, ongoing works, security requirements, hazards, animals, restricted rooms, and known disputes;
* ensure that the Inspector can safely enter and leave the premises;
* identify the areas and fixtures relevant to the Listing and ownership claim;
* arrange lawful access to documents, title information, corporate records, authority letters, and other evidence;
* avoid moving, hiding, altering, or staging material evidence; and
* promptly notify RentDirect of a cancellation, delay, change of address, or change in authority.

RentDirect may postpone, cancel, or refuse a visit where access is unavailable, the property is unsafe, the Landlord’s identity or authority is unclear, the Inspector is threatened or obstructed, a conflict is identified, or the requested inspection would be unlawful or misleading.

## 5. WHAT THE PHYSICAL INSPECTION MAY COVER

Subject to the agreed scope, the Inspector may review visible and reasonably accessible matters including:

* whether the property appears to exist at the stated location;
* whether the visible property broadly corresponds with the Listing description, photographs, property type, rooms, amenities, and stated condition;
* address, access, external identifiers, boundaries or landmarks, and location information;
* occupancy, possession indicators, visible fixtures, utilities, access controls, and material discrepancies;
* material defects, hazards, construction activity, restrictions, or conditions disclosed or observed during the visit; and
* the identity and authority of the person presenting the property.

An inspection is visual and non-invasive unless the written appointment expressly states otherwise. It does not ordinarily include structural, electrical, plumbing, environmental, environmental-health, valuation, surveying, quantity-surveying, building-control, fire-safety, mechanical, or specialist testing. The Inspector may decline to enter a restricted, locked, dangerous, or private area.

## 6. WHAT DOCUMENT REVIEW MAY COVER

Depending on the Listing and the Landlord’s ownership type, the Inspector may review original or reliable copies of documents such as a certificate of occupancy, deed of assignment, governor’s consent, survey plan, approved building or planning evidence, property tax or utility evidence, probate or court documents, corporate records, authority letters, management agreements, or other documents reasonably relevant to the ownership or letting authority.

The Landlord must present complete and legible documents, identify the person or entity to which each document relates, disclose known alterations or gaps, and explain any difference between the document, the Listing, the property, and the Landlord’s identity or authority.

Document review may include checking names, dates, addresses, property descriptions, plot or title references, signatures, stamps, registration references, internal consistency, and available lawful sources. A document being seen, photographed, or recorded does not by itself prove that it is genuine, current, enforceable, registered, free of encumbrances, or sufficient to establish title.

## 7. LAWYER REVIEW AND LIMITS OF AUTHORITY

Only a lawyer who is qualified and authorised to provide the relevant service may give a legal opinion, conduct a title search, advise on enforceability, or issue a legal certification. A lawyer’s appointment for a specific scope does not automatically include a full title search or legal opinion.

An Inspector who is not a lawyer must not describe a visual check or document comparison as a legal title certification. The Inspector must refer any suspected forgery, competing claim, adverse entry, court matter, probate issue, boundary dispute, mortgage, charge, lease, or material legal uncertainty for appropriate legal review.

## 8. EVIDENCE, PHOTOGRAPHS, AND RECORDS

The Inspector may take photographs, notes, timestamps, location evidence, document images, and other records only to the extent reasonably necessary for the verification, safety, audit, dispute, fraud-prevention, or legal purpose. The Inspector must avoid unnecessary capture of people, private belongings, identity numbers, payment information, children, unrelated occupants, and unrelated documents.

RentDirect may retain the report and supporting evidence for the period reasonably required for Platform safety, the Listing, verification history, disputes, fraud prevention, audit, legal claims, and legal obligations. The Landlord must not obstruct lawful recordkeeping or ask the Inspector to delete an accurate record of a material discrepancy.

## 9. VERIFICATION OUTCOMES

The relevant property-verification statuses may include **unverified**, **pending**, or **verified**. RentDirect may also mark a submission as requiring clarification, additional evidence, a second visit, or legal review.

Physical property status and property document status may be recorded separately. A Listing can therefore have a pending or verified physical inspection while its document review remains pending or unverified, or the reverse.

RentDirect may refuse, suspend, withdraw, or change a verification status if information is incomplete, a document cannot be confirmed, the property changes, the Landlord’s authority ends, a later report identifies a risk, or a previous result is no longer reliable.

## 10. NO CERTIFICATION OR GUARANTEE

Unless a separate written legal opinion expressly says otherwise, physical verification is a limited, time-specific risk review. It is not:

* a conveyance, title certificate, survey, valuation, structural report, safety certificate, insurance inspection, or government approval;
* a guarantee that the property is free from a mortgage, charge, lien, adverse claim, tenancy, restriction, defect, or dispute;
* a guarantee that the Landlord owns the property or will perform a tenancy agreement;
* a recommendation, endorsement, or promise that a Tenant should rent the property; or
* a substitute for independent legal, property, engineering, financial, safety, or regulatory due diligence.

The Landlord remains responsible for title, authority, lawful letting, property condition, disclosures, tenancy obligations, taxes, repairs, and all statements in the Listing.

## 11. DISCLOSURES TO USERS

RentDirect may display a proportionate verification badge, status, or summary to eligible Users in accordance with the Platform’s access controls. RentDirect may describe the verification route as document-based or physical/in-person where that description is accurate.

The Landlord must not use the RentDirect badge, report, Inspector’s name, or a partial document image to imply a broader guarantee than the relevant status and scope support. RentDirect may correct, remove, or restrict a verification claim that is stale, misleading, or used outside the Platform.

## 12. FEES, CANCELLATION, AND FAILED VISITS

Any inspection, travel, legal-review, repeat-visit, or related fee must be shown or separately agreed before the Landlord becomes liable for it. The applicable checkout, appointment confirmation, or written engagement controls the amount, tax treatment, cancellation rule, and refund rule.

Unless a separate policy says otherwise, a failed visit caused by inaccurate information, unavailable access, unsafe conditions, obstruction, or the Landlord’s cancellation may require rescheduling and may result in a non-refundable cost that was clearly disclosed in advance. No fee creates an entitlement to a verified status.

## 13. PRIVACY AND CONFIDENTIALITY

The Landlord must provide only information reasonably required for verification and must not submit another person’s personal data without a lawful basis and appropriate notice or consent. RentDirect and the Inspector must handle verification information under the Privacy Policy, the Verification and Screening Consent, and applicable data-protection law.

The Landlord must not publish an Inspector’s personal contact information, identity document, private report, or image without permission. The Inspector and RentDirect may disclose relevant information to authorised reviewers, service providers, affected Users, regulators, law-enforcement bodies, insurers, professional advisers, or courts where permitted or required by law.

## 14. FRAUD, OBSTRUCTION, AND REPORTING

The Landlord must not provide forged, altered, incomplete, or misleading documents, arrange a substitute property or person, conceal a material occupant or dispute, coach an Inspector to record a false result, offer an improper benefit, threaten or harass an Inspector, or interfere with evidence.

Report suspected fraud, unsafe conduct, impersonation, improper payment demands, or data misuse promptly to **info@rentdirect.homes**. RentDirect may pause a Listing or payment, require further evidence, remove a verification status, suspend an account, terminate access, preserve evidence, and report suspected unlawful conduct to an appropriate authority.

## 15. LANDLORD RESPONSIBILITY AND INDEMNITY

To the extent permitted by law, the Landlord is responsible for loss, injury, damage, inaccurate information, unlawful access, or third-party claims arising from the Landlord’s property, instructions, documents, representatives, occupants, or failure to disclose a material risk. The Landlord agrees to indemnify RentDirect and its authorised Inspectors for claims and reasonable costs arising from the Landlord’s breach of these Terms, fraud, lack of authority, or unlawful conduct.

## 16. ACCEPTANCE

By choosing physical or in-person verification, scheduling or confirming an inspection, presenting documents, granting access, publishing the Listing with the physical-verification route, or continuing to use the resulting status, the Landlord acknowledges and accepts these Terms.

Questions or requests concerning this document may be sent to **info@rentdirect.homes**.
